# Stewardship sources

## Stewardship

Sources checked on 2026-11-08 for [STEWARDSHIP.md](../guides/STEWARDSHIP.md). Findings come from full text unless an entry says otherwise. The research measures the cost of configuration, technical debt and deprecation, and how often ideas fail. No source tests a stewardship review procedure. Applying these findings to a PR review is an inference.

### Stewardship research

- **S81. Tianyin Xu, Long Jin, Xuepeng Fan, Yuanyuan Zhou, Shankar Pasupathy and Rukma Talwadker (2015), [Hey, You Have Given Me Too Many Knobs! Understanding and Dealing with Over-Designed Configuration in System Software](https://doi.org/10.1145/2786805.2786852), ESEC/FSE 2015, 307–319. DOI 10.1145/2786805.2786852. [Authors' copy](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~tixu/papers/fse15.pdf).** Research (field study of user settings). Covers thousands of customers of a commercial storage system and hundreds of users of two open-source systems. Only 6.1% to 16.7% of parameters were set by most users, and up to 54.1% were rarely set by anyone. Up to 48.5% of configuration issues were difficulties finding or setting parameters. Their guidelines could hide or remove 51.9% of the commercial system's parameters with little impact on existing users. Limit: system software, not libraries or web APIs.
- **S82. Zuoning Yin, Xiao Ma, Jing Zheng, Yuanyuan Zhou, Lakshmi N. Bairavasundaram and Shankar Pasupathy (2011), [An Empirical Study on Configuration Errors in Commercial and Open Source Systems](https://doi.org/10.1145/2043556.2043572), SOSP 2011, 159–172. DOI 10.1145/2043556.2043572. [Authors' copy](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2011/current/2011-Cascais/12-yin-online.pdf).** Research (546 real misconfigurations from a commercial storage system and four open-source systems). 70.0% to 85.5% were parameter mistakes. Many caused crashes, hangs or severe slowdowns. Supports counting misconfiguration risk as a cost of options. Does not measure how many options a system should have.
- **S83. Iago Abal, Claus Brabrand and Andrzej Wąsowski (2014), [42 Variability Bugs in the Linux Kernel: A Qualitative Analysis](https://doi.org/10.1145/2642937.2642990), ASE 2014, 421–432. DOI 10.1145/2642937.2642990.** Research (qualitative analysis of 42 bugs that appear only under some configurations). Abstract read. Shows that variability increases the complexity of bugs. Limit: one large C codebase, qualitative.
- **S84. Terese Besker, Antonio Martini and Jan Bosch (2019), [Software Developer Productivity Loss Due to Technical Debt: A Replication and Extension Study Examining Developers' Development Work](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jss.2019.06.004), Journal of Systems and Software 156, 41–61. DOI 10.1016/j.jss.2019.06.004. [Authors' copy](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/511450/file/511450_Fulltext.pdf).** Research (longitudinal survey of 43 developers, 16 interviews, replication on an independent dataset). Developers reported losing on average 23% of their working time to technical debt. Extra testing was the most common added activity. Limit: self-reported time, and technical debt is broader than unneeded features.
- **S85. Meir M. Lehman (1980), [Programs, Life Cycles, and Laws of Software Evolution](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1980.11805), Proceedings of the IEEE 68(9), 1060–1076. DOI 10.1109/PROC.1980.11805.** Research (laws inferred from observing large evolving systems). The second law says complexity increases as a program is changed unless work is done to maintain or reduce it. Limit: observational laws about a class of large systems, not a measured cost per feature.

### Practitioner reports

- **S86. Ron Kohavi and Stefan Thomke (2017), [The Surprising Power of Online Experiments](https://hbr.org/2017/09/the-surprising-power-of-online-experiments), Harvard Business Review 95(5), 74–82.** Practice (company experiment data reported in a practitioner magazine, not peer reviewed). At Google and Bing about 10% to 20% of experiments produced positive results. At Microsoft about one third proved effective, one third were neutral and one third were negative. Supports treating a proposer's confidence as weak evidence of value. Limit: consumer web products with A/B testing.
- **S87. Murali Krishna Ramanathan, Lazaro Clapp, Rajkishore Barik and Manu Sridharan (2020), [Piranha: Reducing Feature Flag Debt at Uber](https://doi.org/10.1145/3377813.3381350), ICSE-SEIP 2020. [Uber blog post](https://www.uber.com/blog/piranha/), 17 March 2020.** Practice (industrial experience report). Describes stale flags as technical debt that hurts reliability and maintenance. Reports cleanup changes for 1,381 flags, 17% of all flags, between December 2017 and May 2019. These figures come from the abstract as shown in search results and the conference listing. DOI 10.1145/3377813.3381350, resolved through Crossref.

### Standards

- **S88. ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018, [Systems and software engineering — Life cycle processes — Requirements engineering](https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html).** Standard. Lists "necessary" among the characteristics of an individual requirement. Full text not inspected, and the ISO page refused automated access. The characteristic is confirmed through secondary summaries that follow the standard, such as the INCOSE Guide to Writing Requirements. No claim is made about the clause's exact wording.

### Open-source proposal processes

- **S89. Rust, [RFC template](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/0000-template.md).** Practice. Motivation must explain the problem and give "several specific use cases". Drawbacks asks why the change should not be made. Rationale asks for alternatives and "What is the impact of not doing this?"
- **S90. Python, [PEP 1: PEP Purpose and Guidelines](https://peps.python.org/pep-0001/).** Practice. Says motivation is critical and that proposals without sufficient motivation may be rejected. Asks authors to record rejected ideas and the reasons, so they are not raised again.
- **S91. Go, [Proposing Changes to Go](https://github.com/golang/proposal/blob/master/README.md).** Practice. Aims for a recorded evaluation with a clear answer. Without consensus, a proposal is usually declined. Language changes should address an important issue for many people and have minimal impact on everybody else.
- **S92. Kubernetes, [KEP template](https://github.com/kubernetes/enhancements/blob/master/keps/NNNN-kep-template/README.md).** Practice. Includes Motivation, Goals and Non-Goals, Drawbacks ("Why should this KEP not be implemented?") and Alternatives.

### API design and maintenance practice

- **S93. Joshua Bloch (2006), [How to Design a Good API and Why it Matters](https://www.cin.ufpe.br/~if669/files/MaterialDeEnsino/Referencias/api-design-oopsla-2006.pdf), invited talk, OOPSLA 2006 (slides).** Practice / opinion. "API should be as small as possible but no smaller." "When in doubt leave it out. You can always add, but you can never remove." Limit: experience-based guidance, not measured.
- **S94. Hyrum Wright, [Hyrum's Law](https://www.hyrumslaw.com/).** Practice (observation). With enough users of an API, all observable behaviors will be depended on by somebody, whatever the contract says. Supports planning removal before release.
- **S95. Martin Fowler (2015), [Yagni](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/Yagni.html).** Practice / opinion. Names the cost of build, delay, carry and repair for a presumptive feature. Says YAGNI does not apply to work that makes software easier to modify.
- **S96. Pete Hodgson (2017), [Feature Toggles (aka Feature Flags)](https://martinfowler.com/articles/feature-toggles.html).** Practice. Treats toggles as inventory with a carrying cost and advises keeping it low. Describes removal tasks, expiry dates and caps on the number of toggles.
- **S97. Havoc Pennington (2002), [Free software UI](https://ometer.com/free-software-ui.html).** Opinion (desktop software maintainer). Argues that each preference has a cost: harder discovery, more testing, weaker defaults and less time for real features. Limit: desktop UI, essay.

### Product management and discovery

- **S103. Inge van de Weerd, Sjaak Brinkkemper, Richard Nieuwenhuis, Johan Versendaal and Lex Bijlsma (2006), [Towards a Reference Framework for Software Product Management](https://doi.org/10.1109/RE.2006.66), 14th IEEE International Requirements Engineering Conference (RE 2006). DOI 10.1109/RE.2006.66.** Research (conceptual framework). Abstract as shown in search results and bibliographic records. Names four key process areas of software product management: portfolio management, product roadmapping, release planning and requirements management. Used here to name the field, not as evidence for any principle.

- **S98. Ryan Singer, [Shape Up](https://basecamp.com/shapeup), Basecamp (online book), chapter ["Set Boundaries"](https://basecamp.com/shapeup/1.2-chapter-03).** Practice. Appetite fixes the time a problem is worth before design starts. Advises a soft no for raw ideas and narrowing the problem before designing a solution. Limit: one company's product process.
- **S99. 37signals, [Getting Real](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal), chapter ["Start With No"](https://basecamp.com/gettingreal/05.3-start-with-no).** Practice / opinion. Advises answering feature requests with "not now" by default and making each feature prove itself. Limit: small commercial web products.
- **S100. Clayton M. Christensen, Taddy Hall, Karen Dillon and David S. Duncan (2016), [Know Your Customers' "Jobs to Be Done"](https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done), Harvard Business Review, September 2016.** Practice. Only the public summary was read. Argues that innovation succeeds when it starts from why customers make their choices, not from the product. Limit: consumer and business products.
- **S101. Marty Cagan (2017), [The Four Big Risks](https://www.svpg.com/four-big-risks/), SVPG.** Practice. Names value risk, whether users will choose to use the product, as one of four risks to address before building.

### Compatibility, deprecation and usage

- **S104. Christopher Bogart, Christian Kästner, James Herbsleb and Ferdian Thung (2021), [When and How to Make Breaking Changes: Policies and Practices in 18 Open Source Software Ecosystems](https://doi.org/10.1145/3447245), ACM Transactions on Software Engineering and Methodology 30(4). DOI 10.1145/3447245.** Research (interview case study of Eclipse, npm and CRAN, then a survey, repository mining and document analysis across 18 ecosystems). Abstract read. Ecosystem norms on breaking changes shift the costs of using and maintaining software between stakeholders, according to each ecosystem's values. All ecosystems valued stability and compatibility.
- **S105. Anand Ashok Sawant, Romain Robbes and Alberto Bacchelli (2018), [On the Reaction to Deprecation of Clients of 4 + 1 Popular Java APIs and the JDK](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-017-9554-9), Empirical Software Engineering 23, 2158–2197. DOI 10.1007/s10664-017-9554-9. [Authors' copy](https://anandsaw.github.io/publications/emse2017.pdf).** Research (repository mining of more than 25,000 GitHub clients of five Java APIs and 60 JDK clients). Only a small fraction of clients reacted to deprecation. Very few replaced the deprecated call. Limit: Java, and deprecation policies differ between APIs.
- **S106. Aline Brito, Laerte Xavier, André Hora and Marco Tulio Valente (2018), [Why and How Java Developers Break APIs](https://doi.org/10.1109/SANER.2018.8330214), SANER 2018, 255–265. DOI 10.1109/SANER.2018.8330214. [arXiv 1801.05198](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.05198).** Research (field study, 59 breaking changes confirmed by developers of 19 projects). Abstract read. Breaking changes were mostly motivated by new features, by making APIs simpler with fewer elements, and by maintainability. Limit: small sample of Java libraries.
- **S107. [Semantic Versioning 2.0.0](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).** Standard (community specification). Item 7 requires a minor release when public API functionality is deprecated. The FAQ adds that at least one minor release should contain the deprecation before a major release removes it. Applies to projects that adopt SemVer.
- **S108. Go, [Go 1 and the Future of Go Programs](https://go.dev/doc/go1compat).** Practice (a project's compatibility promise). Programs written to the Go 1 specification should keep compiling and running. Lists exceptions such as security fixes, unspecified behavior and bug fixes. Shows one project's explicit compatibility contract, not a general rule.
- **S109. Hyrum Wright, "Deprecation", chapter 15 of Titus Winters, Tom Manshreck and Hyrum Wright (eds.) (2020), [Software Engineering at Google](https://abseil.io/resources/swe-book/html/ch15.html), O'Reilly.** Practice. States that code is a liability, not an asset. Advisory deprecation with warnings and no deadline rarely moves existing users. Compulsory deprecation with a deadline and a dedicated owner works better. Advises planning for retirement when a system is built. Limit: a large company with a monorepo.
- **S110. Russ Cox (2023), [Transparent Telemetry for Open-Source Projects](https://research.swtch.com/telemetry-intro), research!rsc, 8 February 2023.** Practice / proposal. Argues that bug reports and surveys miss how open-source software is used, and proposes minimal, public usage counters. The post notes that the design became opt-in. Limit: one project's design proposal.

### Evidence not carried forward

- **S102. Mountain Goat Software (Mike Cohn's site, last updated 2016), [Are 64% of Features Really Rarely or Never Used?](https://www.mountaingoatsoftware.com/blog/are-64-of-features-really-rarely-or-never-used).** Practice (critique). The widely repeated claim that 64% of features are rarely or never used comes from a Standish Group talk at XP 2002. The post reports that it was based on four internal applications at four companies, none of them commercial products. We did not find the original data and do not use the figure.
